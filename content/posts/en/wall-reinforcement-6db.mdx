What happens if I point a bass amp's speaker straight at a vertical wall? What's the
mathematical principle by which the low end doubles? Where does the $+6\,\mathrm{dB}$
figure come from?

At low frequencies, where the cone is small compared to the wavelength, the field is
spherical and $p(r) = \frac{A}{r}\,e^{-jkr}$, with $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$.

## Wave equation and linearity

In the linear acoustic regime the pressure $p(\mathbf{x},t)$ in a homogeneous, isotropic,
lossless medium satisfies the wave equation:

$$
\nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2} = 0
$$

The associated operator $\mathcal{L}[p] = \nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{c^2}\,\partial_t^2 p$ is
**linear**: superposition holds, which is the foundation of interference.

### Separation of variables

Looking for a separable solution $p(\mathbf{x},t) = X(\mathbf{x})\,T(t)$ and dividing by
$XT$ yields two decoupled equations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ddot{T} + \omega^2 T &= 0 \\
\nabla^2 X + k^2 X &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The latter is the **Helmholtz equation**, with $k = \dfrac{\omega}{c} = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}$.

## Where the +6 dB comes from

Near an ideal rigid wall (infinite impedance, zero normal velocity) the problem is
equivalent to an in-phase **image source**. When the cone-to-wall distance is much smaller
than $\lambda/4$, the two waves arrive in phase and with equal amplitude. Since the sound
pressure level is

$$
\mathrm{SPL} = 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{p}{p_0}\right),
$$

if the pressure doubles:

$$
\Delta\,\mathrm{SPL} = 20\log_{10}(2) \approx \SI{6.02}{dB}.
$$

So the reinforcement comes from:

- linearity of the wave equation;
- the Neumann boundary condition at the wall;
- selection of outgoing solutions (Sommerfeld);
- coherent interference with the image source.

It is not a broadband effect: as frequency rises the delays become comparable to
$\lambda/4$ and the reinforcement turns into *comb filtering*.

## The image-source model

The rigid wall is equivalent to an **image source** $S'$, mirrored and in phase with the
real source $S$: this is the construction that satisfies the Neumann condition at the wall.
